How Does Google Analytics 4 Track Users Without Cookies?

By Jane Smith, Head of Product at Acme Analytics — Published January 15, 2026 · Updated March 20, 2026 · 8 min read
Analytics Privacy Cookieless
Short answer: GA4 doesn't fully track without cookies. It uses first-party cookies for session tracking, then fills gaps with User-ID (logged-in users), Google Signals (cross-device), and machine learning modeling for users who decline consent.

When Google announced GA4 as "cookieless-ready," it caused widespread confusion. Does GA4 actually work without cookies? The answer is: partly. GA4 reduces cookie dependency but does not eliminate it. Here's exactly how each tracking mechanism works.

The 4 Ways GA4 Tracks Users

1. First-Party Cookies (Default)

By default, GA4 still writes a first-party cookie (_ga) to your domain to identify returning visitors. This cookie is set for 2 years and is readable only by your domain — not Google's. First-party cookies are less affected by browser restrictions than third-party cookies, but they still require consent under GDPR.

2. User-ID (Logged-In Users)

If your site has user authentication, you can pass a stable User-ID to GA4. This ID persists across devices and browsers, letting GA4 stitch together a complete user journey. User-ID tracking is the most accurate method but only works for authenticated users — typically 20-40% of your total traffic.

3. Google Signals (Cross-Device)

Google Signals links sessions across devices when users are signed into a Google account. It is opt-in at the property level and requires users to have enabled "Ad personalization." This is the most powerful cross-device signal but depends entirely on Google account sign-in.

4. Modeling & Blended Data

For users who decline consent, GA4 (with Consent Mode v2) fills data gaps using machine learning models trained on consenting users with similar characteristics. Google calls this "behavioral modeling." It's disclosed in aggregate reports with a modeling indicator.

Comparison: GA4 Tracking Methods

Method Requires Consent? Works Without Login? Cross-Device?
First-party cookie Yes (GDPR) Yes No
User-ID Yes No (login required) Yes
Google Signals Yes No (Google login) Yes
Modeling No Yes Partial

What This Means for Your Analytics Setup

If you need fully cookieless, consent-free analytics, GA4 alone won't get you there. You'll either need to implement Consent Mode v2 (with its modeling limitations) or use a cookieless analytics alternative like Plausible, Fathom, or Acme Analytics.

Conclusion

GA4 is more privacy-friendly than Universal Analytics but still relies on cookies by default. For complete cookieless tracking, you need either User-ID (logged-in users only) or a third-party tool designed around cookieless measurement. The "cookieless future" GA4 was designed for is a future where third-party cookies are gone — not first-party cookies.
